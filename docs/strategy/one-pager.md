# Kalpi — Strategy one-pager

**Working title:** Kalpi / קלפי (ballot box + playing cards).
**Product:** Free digital collect-only TCG of election lists, members, symbols, and platforms.
**North star:** אוריינות פוליטית — a **smart vote**. Get them to the kalpi *and* make the slip informed.
**Subgoal:** Stay objective. The game never picks a side.
**Pitch line:** The dopamine is the bait. Civic literacy and turnout are the product. The ends justify the empty means.
**Alignment sheet:** [alignment.md](../product/alignment.md)

---

## Audience

**Primary:** Israelis 18–24, especially the first-time cohort (18–22) voting for the 26th Knesset.

- **~595,000–640,000** first-time voters (Points Location Intelligence / IDI citing CBS).
- About **8.7% of the electorate** — on the order of **six Knesset seats** if they turn out.
- Secondary later: 25–29, soldiers, anyone who learns politics from the feed.

They already live in pack-rips and short-form video. They do not live in party PDFs.

What is actually going on with them (not a single “won’t vote” story):

| Pattern | Product response |
|---|---|
| Many are **new** voters | Teach lists and the booth, not assume they already know |
| They may vote the **parents’ default** | Facts they can use without a family fight |
| They may **not care** — war, load, enough already | 90-second rip, not a lecture |
| **TikTok / viral fake news** is often their civics class | Sourced backs + commons. Knowledge is how we fight the fake, not clip-by-clip debunking |
| Some are **afraid to show an opinion** (at least on one side) | Camp pledge is **anonymous**. Energy is a tile, not a name |

Parents are the wrong teacher for this age: **rebellion**, and parents are **invested / emotional**. The syllabus is the card back.

Lived pitch examples (not stats): “Bennett is a bro” (Guy); little brother; Hadar Muchtar — three rooms that do not know who to vote for.

## What we can prove

### Turnout gap is real

After the 2021 elections, the Knesset Research and Information Center reported turnout rising with age: **46.7% among ages 18–24 vs 74.9% among ages 65–74**. National turnout in 2022 was **70.6%**. Young adults vote less than older Israelis. That is the demographic we can defend.

Lavi & Adler (Tel Aviv–Yaffo Academic College): young Israelis vote less than older Israelis, more than young Europeans, and youth turnout has been rising. We are not inventing a collapse. We are targeting a **persistent age gap**.

### Intention surveys run hot. Official youth turnout does not.

IDI’s May 2026 first-time-voter survey (n=576, ages 18–22) found **80–95% intend to vote**. That number, by itself, does not forecast October.

We do not have a published 18–24 intention series for every past cycle that we can subtract from 46.7%. We **do** have the Israeli pattern:

- Before the **2013** elections, IDI found **~88%** certain or likely to vote (72.8% certain + 15% probably). After the vote, **~92%** of respondents said they had voted. Official turnout was **67.8%** ([IDI, Who Doesn’t Vote](https://en.idi.org.il/articles/5204)).
- In that same study, **18–22** were already the age group most likely to say they would stay home (**17.7%**) and most likely to report afterwards that they had not voted (**~15%**), versus 7–9% in older groups.
- Vote-intention and post-election “I voted” answers are a known overreport worldwide, including in Israeli municipal work (Waismel-Manor & Sarid, 2011). Social desirability + politically involved survey samples.

**Working assumption for the pitch:** official 18–24 turnout has been low. A 2026 intention figure in the 80s–90s is what surveys usually look like. Treat 2026 youth turnout as **still low** versus older adults and versus the national rate — even if it ticks a few points above 46.7%. Do not treat IDI 2026 as proof they will show.

The second job, on top of that: **informed slip**. Feed politics, low seeking, weak trust, low knowledge of lists and platforms.

### Knowledge claim — frame it this way

We do **not** have a clean “X% of 18–24 cannot name three parties” number. Do not invent one.

We **do** have: digital/social as the #1 information source, passive feed exposure, distrust of parties, and a civics system that has long struggled to turn facts into usable political literacy.

**Product thesis: אוריינות פוליטית. Get them to the kalpi, and make the slip informed. Not “kids are stupid.”**

Jam goals we also owe (knowledge, social involvement, encourage voting, taking choices, fight AI fake news via knowledge, young audience): see [alignment.md](../product/alignment.md).

## Bait vs goal

```
Daily pack  →  flip the back  →  learn the list
     ↓
Holo / numbered hit  →  share  →  chase the set
     ↓
Informed vote  →  show up 27 Oct 2026
```

The pack is bait. The **goal** is to teach Israeli politics well enough to induce a smart vote — and show-up. The card back is **one** lesson, not the only one. Quiz on a face you already pulled, commons, party pack, streaks: same job.

A beautiful rip with no learning beat is a failed civic game.

**Progression is bound to learning.** Extra packs come from a 5-minute swipe quiz on a card you already own — not from spending, not from pledging a camp, not from grinding empty rips.

## המטרה מקדשת את האמצעים

Game jams usually reward lore, heart, a message in the fiction. Kalpi is deliberately thin there: daily dopamine, no story bible, viral loops that can look “empty.”

That is the point. The civic goal is the message. We use the cheap, fast, TikTok-native means — pack rips, holos, party energy boards, AI art — because 27 October will not wait for a novel. Say this in the room before someone calls it soulless.

On that slide: **QR to prior jams** — [guygir.itch.io](https://guygir.itch.io). I have shipped jam games. This time the emptiness is a choice, not a skill gap.

## Non-goals

- No lore, no invented plot, no *fictional* factions. Players may pledge a **real list** as a camp (see GDD). The game still does not pick one.
- No PvP, no duels, no deck-building combat.
- No monetization, no IAP, no pay-to-complete.
- No endorsing a party. Equal card structure for every list.
- No dark-pattern spending. No cash-prize Alpha.

## North-star metrics (deck, not Alpha)

Habit

- D1 / D7 return after the first pack.

Learning

- % of pulled cards that get **flipped to the back**.
- Quiz accuracy on cards the player owns (timed swipe popups).
- Extra packs earned from quizzes vs from login (learning share of progression).
- Set-completion % (party sets + commons).

Outcome

- Self-reported “I know who I’m voting for.”
- Post-election turnout if a research partner can measure it.

Vanity metrics (pulls, holos shared) are allowed only as **leading indicators** of the learning loop.
