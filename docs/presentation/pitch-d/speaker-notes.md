## 2 — What we can prove · why this cohort

Stand on **46.7 vs 74.9**. Knesset Research and Information Center, after 2021. National 2022: **70.6%**. Say the **28-point** gap out loud.

The gap is the issue, not a collapse. Lavi & Adler (Tel Aviv–Yaffo): young Israelis vote *more* than young Europeans; the youth rate has been rising; they still vote far less than older Israelis. We are targeting a **persistent age gap**.

**595–640k** first-timers, ages 18–22. About **8.7%** of the electorate. On the order of **six Knesset seats** if they turn out. Points / JPost and IDI citing CBS — use the range, not a single fake precision.

**Why they are still the target** (do not skip): lowest turnout, largest new bloc, weakest list-knowledge. Older voters already show up. This group can move six seats without knowing who sits in slots 1–20.

Calendar we build to: lists **9 September**, election **27 October**, threshold **3.25%**, 120 seats, government 61. Virality needs weeks. A launch the week of the vote is too late.

If someone says “youth always vote less”: yes. That is why they are the target.

## 3 — Surveys are not turnout · two jobs

IDI May 2026, n=576, ages 18–22: **80–95% intend** to vote. Hold it. It is not a forecast.

Same institute, **2013**: ~**88%** certain or likely (72.8% + 15%). After the vote, ~**92%** of respondents said they had voted. Official turnout: **67.8%**. [IDI, Who Doesn’t Vote](https://en.idi.org.il/articles/5204).

In that study, **18–22** were already the age group most likely to say they would stay home (**17.7%**) and most likely to report afterwards that they had not (**~15%**), versus 7–9% in older groups.

Vote-intention overreport is a known bias (social desirability, politically involved samples), including Israeli municipal work (Waismel-Manor & Sarid, 2011).

**Working assumption:** official 18–24 turnout has been low. A 2026 figure in the 80s–90s is what surveys usually look like. Treat 2026 youth turnout as still low versus older adults — even if a few points above 46.7%.

**Two jobs:** get them there, and make the slip informed. Feed is #1 political source (IDI 2026). Most do not seek. We do **not** have “X% cannot name three parties.” Do not invent one. Say: they do not know the *list*.

Forbidden: “IDI proves 91% will vote.” “Kids are stupid.” “Young Israelis will not vote, period.”

## 14 — Three choice mechanics

**Energy does not exist.** There is no currency. If someone says “energy,” correct it.

**1 — in-game faction.** Optional. Pick one real list *in the game*. Anonymous (tile, not name). Each pack you open adds **+1** to that list’s public tally. The number is “packs this faction’s players opened.” Not a poll. Not a wallet. **No extra pack. No prize.** People come back so their list’s number moves. Switch allowed. Unfactioned players still play; their packs do not tally. Same tile size. Order = CEC letter / alpha, not polls.

Afraid to show an opinion? This is the bypass.

**2 — real-life party work.** Someone verifies they volunteered at a **party HQ, campaign, or kalpi**. That is the best way to become knowledgeable — they are in the room. So it pays a **pack bundle**. Does **not** auto-join the in-game faction.

**3 — survey → party pack.** Optional. Issues, not “who is a bro.” Server scores a **published table** (neutrality — same questions for everyone). Reward: politicians from the matched list only. Copy: “Based on what you marked, here is that set. Then collect the others.” Tool, not endorsement. Never hides the rest of the binder. Never auto-factions. Survey items + table are still an open task.

If they mix 1 and 2, stop and separate them.

## 15 — List-slot numbered holos

Life TCG prints holos to an animal’s wild population. We print to **official list slot**, same rule on every list.

- Slot 1 → **1/1**
- Slot 2 → 1/2, 2/2
- Slot 3 → 1/3, 2/3, 3/3
- Slot n → 1/n … n/n

Likud 1 and Democrats 1 and Ra'am 1 are all 1/1. We are teaching list order, not poll share. Server assigns the number. Never polls, never seats, never “importance.”

Symbol / platform / quote / commons are **not** slot-numbered.

**Daily date** on a holo (`01.09.2026`) is a separate treatment — screenshot caption, not a second print-run system. Slot-number is the chase.
