# Kalpi — PoC card set (28 cards)

Equal structure: **3 parties × (4 members + 1 symbol + 2 platform + 1 quote) + 4 commons.**

All list slots are **as reported ahead of the 9 September 2026 Central Elections Committee deadline**. They can still move. That uncertainty is itself a teaching point.

Word counts are body-only. Target: members 48–56, symbols 46–54, platforms 48–56, quotes 42–50, commons 50–58.

## Shared commons (SYS)

### SYS-C-01 — The Knesset · הכנסת
- **Type:** Commons · **Rarity:** Common
- **Subtitle:** 120 seats · one chamber · one country-wide ballot
- **Body:** Israel’s parliament has 120 members in a single chamber. You do not elect a local MK. The whole country is one constituency. Seats are divided among lists that clear the threshold, in proportion to their valid votes. A government needs 61 seats.
- **Why it matters:** You are voting for a list’s share of 120, not for a neighborhood representative.
- **Source:** Basic Law: The Knesset, art. 4; Knesset electoral-system lexicon.

### SYS-C-02 — The Kalpi · הקלפי
- **Type:** Commons · **Rarity:** Uncommon · **Hero card**
- **Subtitle:** The ballot box · you vote for a list
- **Body:** At the kalpi you take one paper slip for one submitted list and put it in the box. Legally you vote for a closed list, not a person. If that list wins 18 seats, the first 18 names on it enter the Knesset, unless someone resigns later. The slip is secret.
- **Why it matters:** Knowing who sits in slots 1–20 is how you know who your vote can actually seat.
- **Source:** Knesset Elections Law (combined version), 1969; IDI “How Votes Become Knesset Seats.”

### SYS-C-03 — The Threshold · אחוז החסימה
- **Type:** Commons · **Rarity:** Rare / daily-holo candidate · **Hero card**
- **Subtitle:** 3.25% of valid votes
- **Body:** Since 2015 a list needs 3.25% of valid votes to take any seats. Votes for lists under the line stay “valid” on the tally, but they elect nobody. In 2022 the seat quota after the threshold was about 36,227 votes. Small lists near the line can move several seats between blocs.
- **Why it matters:** A protest slip that misses 3.25% does not become a protest MK.
- **Source:** IDI explainer, 2026; Knesset 25th-election results.

### SYS-C-04 — Central Elections Committee · ועדת הבחירות המרכזית
- **Type:** Commons · **Rarity:** Common
- **Subtitle:** Lists due 9 Sept 2026 · election 27 Oct 2026
- **Body:** The CEC runs the election: it takes the lists, assigns ballot letters, and publishes results. Parties must file candidate lists by 9 September 2026. Israelis abroad vote at missions from 20 October. Election day is Tuesday 27 October 2026. Results are confirmed later.
- **Why it matters:** Until 9 September, every “final list” you see in a pack is still a report, not a filed slate.
- **Source:** CEC calendar; JPost election-dates explainer, 13 July 2026.

## Likud (LIK)

### LIK-M-01 — Benjamin Netanyahu · בנימין נתניהו
- **Type:** Member · **Rarity:** Rare
- **Subtitle:** Slot 1 · Likud chair · Prime Minister
- **Body:** Netanyahu heads the Likud list and the current government. The 26th Knesset election on 27 October 2026 will choose the coalition that succeeds that government. Likud is running as a national-liberal right list. Several high slots on the 2026 slate are reserved for the chair’s appointments and were still marked TBD in late August.
- **Why it matters:** A vote for Likud is a vote to keep this list, and this chair, at the center of coalition math.
- **Source:** Wikipedia party lists (citing Ynet, 18 Aug 2026); 2026 election overview.

### LIK-M-02 — Eli Cohen · אלי כהן
- **Type:** Member · **Rarity:** Uncommon
- **Subtitle:** Slot 2 · as reported after Likud primaries
- **Body:** Eli Cohen placed second on the emerging Likud slate after the August 2026 primaries. He is a sitting Likud minister and MK. The party’s primary fills most national slots; the chair still names several reserved places. Slot 2 is therefore an elected place, not an appointment.
- **Why it matters:** If Likud wins two or more seats, this is the second name your slip can seat.
- **Source:** Ynet / Srugim primary tallies, 18 Aug 2026; party-list compilation.

### LIK-M-03 — Israel Katz · ישראל כץ
- **Type:** Member · **Rarity:** Common
- **Subtitle:** Slot 4 · senior Likud minister
- **Body:** Israel Katz appears in the fourth realistic slot on the reported 2026 Likud list. He is a long-serving Likud MK and a senior minister in the current government. The slot above him (3) was still listed as a chair appointment in late August, so Katz is the next elected name after Cohen.
- **Why it matters:** Senior ministers in the first ten names are the people a large Likud vote actually returns to the table.
- **Source:** Party-list compilation from Ynet primary results, 18 Aug 2026.

### LIK-M-04 — Amir Ohana · אמיר אוחנה
- **Type:** Member · **Rarity:** Common
- **Subtitle:** Slot 6 · Speaker of the Knesset
- **Body:** Amir Ohana is Speaker of the Knesset and appears in the sixth slot on the reported Likud list. The Speaker runs the plenum and is a public face of the sitting Knesset. His 2026 list place is a primary slot, not one of the chair’s reserved appointments.
- **Why it matters:** You are not only voting for a prime minister. You are seating the people who run the chamber.
- **Source:** Party-list compilation from Ynet primary results, 18 Aug 2026.

### LIK-S-01 — Ballot mark מחל · Hero card
- **Type:** Symbol · **Rarity:** Uncommon
- **Subtitle:** Likud’s familiar ballot letters
- **Body:** Israeli ballots identify lists by letters, not by a portrait. Likud has long used the letters מחל. The CEC assigns or confirms letters when lists are filed. Until 9 September 2026, treat any letter as the historically associated mark, not as the filed 2026 slip.
- **Why it matters:** In the booth you will look for letters, not for a name you saw on television.
- **Source:** CEC ballot practice; Knesset election lexicon.

### LIK-P-01 — Security and national identity
- **Type:** Platform · **Rarity:** Common
- **Subtitle:** Party line · not a new 2026 PDF
- **Body:** Likud’s published 2026 materials emphasize continuity rather than a fresh manifesto page: Netanyahu-led security policy, Jewish national identity, and a right-wing coalition economy. Independent trackers note no dedicated new platform page on the party site, and an official “government achievements” document instead.
- **Why it matters:** A Likud slip is a vote for continuity of this security and identity line, not for an unreleased new program.
- **Source:** israel2026.co.il party tracker (Likud program status).

### LIK-P-02 — “Broad national government”
- **Type:** Platform · **Rarity:** Common
- **Subtitle:** 2026 campaign frame
- **Body:** In the 2026 campaign Likud has used the slogan of a “broad national government.” Reporting describes this as a shift toward consensus language aimed at voters tired of narrow coalitions, while the practical program remains a right-wing security and governance line. It is messaging, not a filed coalition contract.
- **Why it matters:** Campaign frames are not seat math. 61 still has to be assembled after the count.
- **Source:** Campaign reporting on Likud’s 2026 slogan; israel2026.co.il tracker.

### LIK-Q-01 — Quote, Benjamin Netanyahu
- **Type:** Quote · **Rarity:** Rare
- **Subtitle:** Public line · campaign period 2026
- **Body:** Quote: Likud is campaigning on forming a “broad national government.” That phrase is the party’s own 2026 frame for who it says it wants at the table. It is a slogan, not a signed coalition. Kalpi prints it as a quote so it is not mistaken for a forecast.
- **Why it matters:** Separate what a list claims from what 61 seats will allow.
- **Source:** Likud 2026 campaign slogan, as reported.

## The Democrats (DEM)

### DEM-M-01 — Yair Golan · יאיר גולן
- **Type:** Member · **Rarity:** Rare
- **Subtitle:** Slot 1 · party chair
- **Body:** Yair Golan chairs The Democrats, the 2024 merger of Labor and Meretz, and holds slot 1. He is a former deputy IDF chief and a sitting opposition MK. In July 2026 more than 97,000 members voted in a digital primary (about 86% turnout). Golan also holds reserved appointment slots on the slate.
- **Why it matters:** A Democrats slip seats this chair first, and it seats a merger list, not the old Labor or Meretz names alone.
- **Source:** JPost / Ynet News, Democrats primary, July 2026.

### DEM-M-02 — Naama Lazimi · נעמה לזימי
- **Type:** Member · **Rarity:** Uncommon
- **Subtitle:** Slot 2 · elected in the digital primary
- **Body:** Naama Lazimi, a sitting Democrats MK, won the first elected place behind the chair in the July 2026 primary. More than 97,000 of about 112,000 registered members voted by phone. The party uses a zipper for gender parity on the published slate.
- **Why it matters:** Slot 2 is the first name members chose, not a reserved appointment.
- **Source:** JPost, 2026; Ynet News primary report.

### DEM-M-03 — Gilad Kariv · גלעד קריב
- **Type:** Member · **Rarity:** Common
- **Subtitle:** Slot 3 · MK · Reform rabbi
- **Body:** Gilad Kariv placed third on the Democrats list after the 2026 primary. He is a sitting MK and a Reform rabbi. After the vote he argued that Democrats, not Likud, were “the true major democratic party in Israel,” in a dispute over how Likud was handling its own primaries. That line is his, not Kalpi’s.
- **Why it matters:** Slot 3 is a realistic seat if the list clears a small handful of mandates.
- **Source:** JPost primary report, 2026.

### DEM-M-04 — Efrat Rayten · אפרת רייטן
- **Type:** Member · **Rarity:** Common
- **Subtitle:** Slot 4 · sitting MK
- **Body:** Efrat Rayten placed fourth on the Democrats slate. She is a sitting MK and one of the list’s more visible speakers. Reporting on the party’s 2026 line has quoted her on pairing security with rolling back the current government’s record. Kalpi records her slot and role, not that argument.
- **Why it matters:** The first four names are the people a modest Democrats result actually sends to the Knesset.
- **Source:** JPost / Ynet News slate, July 2026.

### DEM-S-01 — Democrats ballot mark
- **Type:** Symbol · **Rarity:** Uncommon
- **Subtitle:** אמת · requested at filing
- **Body:** The Democrats submitted the list name “הדמוקרטים בראשות יאיר גולן” on 7 September 2026 and requested the ballot letters אמת, continuing Labor’s historical letters rather than Meretz’s מרצ. Submission is not final CEC approval; approval was scheduled for 27 September.
- **Why it matters:** אמת is the mark the party requested for the booth, but the final CEC list remains the authority.
- **Source:** 2026 submitted-list report; CEC approval-status notice.

### DEM-P-01 — October 7 state commission of inquiry
- **Type:** Platform · **Rarity:** Common
- **Subtitle:** Published 2026 agenda item
- **Body:** Democrats materials and launch reporting put a state commission of inquiry into the 7 October 2023 failures at the front of the 2026 program. The party presents this as a first act in any government it joins. Kalpi states the demand, not a verdict on who should sit on such a commission.
- **Why it matters:** This is one concrete test of what a Democrats seat is being asked to do.
- **Source:** Party agenda via israel2026.co.il; campaign reporting on Golan’s launch list.

### DEM-P-02 — Civil status and equal service
- **Type:** Platform · **Rarity:** Common
- **Subtitle:** Published 2026 agenda item
- **Body:** The Democrats’ published agenda includes civil marriage, Shabbat public transport by local choice, and “service for all,” meaning an end to the broad Haredi exemption. These are listed as liberal-Zionist domestic planks alongside rule-of-law items. They are party positions, not current law.
- **Why it matters:** These planks are the domestic half of the list, next to the inquiry plank.
- **Source:** Democrats ideological agenda, as summarized by israel2026.co.il.

### DEM-Q-01 — Quote, Yair Golan
- **Type:** Quote · **Rarity:** Rare
- **Subtitle:** Opening of the 2026 primaries
- **Body:** Quote: “This is the time for the public to go out and vote. Only a strong Democrats team in the Knesset and in the next government can save the country and restore to Israel the security, democracy, and hope that it so desperately needs.” Spoken as party chair at the primary opening.
- **Why it matters:** This is the chair’s ask. Compare it with the two platform cards, not with our voice.
- **Source:** JPost, Democrats primary report, 2026.

## Ra'am — United Arab List (RAM)

### RAM-M-01 — Mansour Abbas · מנסור עבאס
- **Type:** Member · **Rarity:** Rare
- **Subtitle:** Slot 1 · Ra'am chair
- **Body:** Mansour Abbas chairs Ra'am (United Arab List) and was elected to slot 1 at the 22 August 2026 conference in Tayibe. In 2021 Ra'am became the first Arab party to sit as a formal coalition partner (Bennett–Lapid). In 2026 Abbas is running the list separately from the re-formed Joint List.
- **Why it matters:** A Ra'am slip is a vote for this chair’s coalition-flexible, civic-services line — not for the Joint List.
- **Source:** Ynet News, Ra'am list conference; JPost Arab-list explainer, 2026.

### RAM-M-02 — Yoav Segalovitz · יואב סגלוביץ
- **Type:** Member · **Rarity:** Uncommon
- **Subtitle:** Expected slot 2 · first Jewish candidate on the slate
- **Body:** On 31 August 2026 Abbas announced that former Yesh Atid MK Yoav Segalovitz would run with Ra'am. Reporting says he is expected in slot 2, after leaving Yesh Atid. He would be the first Jewish candidate on this list. The CEC filing on 9 September is still the legal text.
- **Why it matters:** If filed at slot 2, this is who a two-seat Ra'am result seats beside Abbas.
- **Source:** Times of Israel / JPost / JTA, 31 Aug–1 Sept 2026.

### RAM-M-03 — Walid Taha · וליד טאהא
- **Type:** Member · **Rarity:** Common
- **Subtitle:** Conference slot behind the chair
- **Body:** Walid Taha was chosen immediately after Abbas at Ra'am’s 28th conference in Tayibe on 22 August 2026, before the Segalovitz announcement. He is a sitting Ra'am MK. Later executive-committee decisions, including an outside candidate in slot 2, can still reorder names before filing.
- **Why it matters:** Conference order is not the filed list. Read the CEC slate on 9 September.
- **Source:** Ynet News, Ra'am conference; Wikipedia 2026 Ra'am primary.

### RAM-M-04 — Iman Khatib-Yasin · אימאן חטיב-יאסין
- **Type:** Member · **Rarity:** Common
- **Subtitle:** Reserved women’s slot on the conference list
- **Body:** Iman Khatib-Yasin, a sitting Ra'am MK, was placed in the conference’s reserved women’s slot (reported as fourth). The executive committee was left to fill slot six onward and to approve outside candidates. Women’s reserved places are part of how this list is built, not a side note.
- **Why it matters:** Reserved slots are a rule of the list, the same way Likud’s chair appointments are a rule of that list.
- **Source:** Ynet News, Ra'am conference list.

### RAM-S-01 — Ballot mark עם
- **Type:** Symbol · **Rarity:** Uncommon
- **Subtitle:** United Arab List · historically עם
- **Body:** Ra'am is the United Arab List. On recent ballots it has used the letters עם. Letters are confirmed when the CEC accepts the 2026 filing. A Joint List slip, if that alliance appears, will be a different mark. Do not mix the two envelopes.
- **Why it matters:** Two Arab lists can be on the same table. The letters are how you tell them apart under time pressure.
- **Source:** CEC ballot practice; JPost 2026 Joint List vs Ra'am explainer.

### RAM-P-01 — Civic services: crime, housing, municipalities
- **Type:** Platform · **Rarity:** Common
- **Subtitle:** Published participation line
- **Body:** Ra'am’s 2026 line, as reported, stresses practical issues in Arab society: crime, housing, infrastructure, and budgets for Arab municipalities. Abbas has argued that Arab citizens can use Knesset participation and coalition talks to move those files. Kalpi records the priorities, not a scorecard of past delivery.
- **Why it matters:** This is what the list says a Ra'am seat is for, day to day.
- **Source:** JPost, “What do the Joint Arab List and Ra’am stand for?”, 2026.

### RAM-P-02 — Coalition flexibility, separate from the Joint List
- **Type:** Platform · **Rarity:** Common
- **Subtitle:** 2026 structural choice
- **Body:** Hadash, Ta'al, and Balad re-formed a Joint List in August 2026. Ra'am is running on its own. Reporting describes Abbas as keeping room to negotiate with more than one bloc, as in 2021. A vote for Ra'am is therefore not a vote for the Joint List’s opposition posture.
- **Why it matters:** Same community, two list strategies. The slip you pick is the strategy you seat.
- **Source:** JPost 2026 explainer; Times of Israel, Segalovitz announcement.

### RAM-Q-01 — Quote, Mansour Abbas
- **Type:** Quote · **Rarity:** Rare
- **Subtitle:** Nazareth, 31 August 2026
- **Body:** Quote: “Hope won out” over fear — Abbas, welcoming Yoav Segalovitz to the list in Nazareth. The line is about that recruitment, on that night. It is not a platform paragraph and not a coalition promise. Kalpi prints it as a dated quote.
- **Why it matters:** Dated quotes expire. Platforms and filed slots last longer.
- **Source:** JTA, 31 Aug 2026; Times of Israel, 1 Sept 2026.
