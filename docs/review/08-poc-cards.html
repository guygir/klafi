Shared commons (SYS)

SYS-C-01 — The Knesset · הכנסת

  • Type: Commons · Rarity: Common
  • Subtitle: 120 seats · one chamber · one country-wide ballot
  • Body: Israel’s parliament has 120 members in a single chamber. You do not elect a local MK. The whole country is one constituency. Seats are divided among lists that clear the threshold, in proportion to their valid votes. A government needs 61 seats.
  • Why it matters: You are voting for a list’s share of 120, not for a neighborhood representative.
  • Source: Basic Law: The Knesset, art. 4; Knesset electoral-system lexicon.

SYS-C-02 — The Kalpi · הקלפי

  • Type: Commons · Rarity: Uncommon · Hero card
  • Subtitle: The ballot box · you vote for a list
  • Body: At the kalpi you take one paper slip for one submitted list and put it in the box. Legally you vote for a closed list, not a person. If that list wins 18 seats, the first 18 names on it enter the Knesset, unless someone resigns later. The slip is secret.
  • Why it matters: Knowing who sits in slots 1–20 is how you know who your vote can actually seat.
  • Source: Knesset Elections Law (combined version), 1969; IDI “How Votes Become Knesset Seats.”

SYS-C-03 — The Threshold · אחוז החסימה

  • Type: Commons · Rarity: Rare / daily-holo candidate · Hero card
  • Subtitle: 3.25% of valid votes
  • Body: Since 2015 a list needs 3.25% of valid votes to take any seats. Votes for lists under the line stay “valid” on the tally, but they elect nobody. In 2022 the seat quota after the threshold was about 36,227 votes. Small lists near the line can move several seats between blocs.
  • Why it matters: A protest slip that misses 3.25% does not become a protest MK.
  • Source: IDI explainer, 2026; Knesset 25th-election results.

SYS-C-04 — Central Elections Committee · ועדת הבחירות המרכזית

  • Type: Commons · Rarity: Common
  • Subtitle: Lists due 9 Sept 2026 · election 27 Oct 2026
  • Body: The CEC runs the election: it takes the lists, assigns ballot letters, and publishes results. Parties must file candidate lists by 9 September 2026. Israelis abroad vote at missions from 20 October. Election day is Tuesday 27 October 2026. Results are confirmed later.
  • Why it matters: Until 9 September, every “final list” you see in a pack is still a report, not a filed slate.
  • Source: CEC calendar; JPost election-dates explainer, 13 July 2026.

LIK-S-01 — Ballot mark מחל · Hero card

  • Type: Symbol · Rarity: Uncommon
  • Subtitle: Likud’s familiar ballot letters
  • Body: Israeli ballots identify lists by letters, not by a portrait. Likud has long used the letters מחל. The CEC assigns or confirms letters when lists are filed. Until 9 September 2026, treat any letter as the historically associated mark, not as the filed 2026 slip.
  • Why it matters: In the booth you will look for letters, not for a name you saw on television.
  • Source: CEC ballot practice; Knesset election lexicon.

LIK-P-01 — Security and national identity

  • Type: Platform · Rarity: Common
  • Subtitle: Party line · not a new 2026 PDF
  • Body: Likud’s published 2026 materials emphasize continuity rather than a fresh manifesto page: Netanyahu-led security policy, Jewish national identity, and a right-wing coalition economy. Independent trackers note no dedicated new platform page on the party site, and an official “government achievements” document instead.
  • Why it matters: A Likud slip is a vote for continuity of this security and identity line, not for an unreleased new program.
  • Source: israel2026.co.il party tracker (Likud program status).

LIK-P-02 — “Broad national government”

  • Type: Platform · Rarity: Common
  • Subtitle: 2026 campaign frame
  • Body: In the 2026 campaign Likud has used the slogan of a “broad national government.” Reporting describes this as a shift toward consensus language aimed at voters tired of narrow coalitions, while the practical program remains a right-wing security and governance line. It is messaging, not a filed coalition contract.
  • Why it matters: Campaign frames are not seat math. 61 still has to be assembled after the count.
  • Source: Campaign reporting on Likud’s 2026 slogan; israel2026.co.il tracker.

LIK-Q-01 — Quote, Benjamin Netanyahu

  • Type: Quote · Rarity: Rare
  • Subtitle: Public line · campaign period 2026
  • Body: Quote: Likud is campaigning on forming a “broad national government.” That phrase is the party’s own 2026 frame for who it says it wants at the table. It is a slogan, not a signed coalition. Kalpi prints it as a quote so it is not mistaken for a forecast.
  • Why it matters: Separate what a list claims from what 61 seats will allow.
  • Source: Likud 2026 campaign slogan, as reported.

The Democrats (DEM)

DEM-M-01 — Yair Golan · יאיר גולן

  • Type: Member · Rarity: Rare
  • Subtitle: Slot 1 · party chair
  • Body: Yair Golan chairs The Democrats, the 2024 merger of Labor and Meretz, and holds slot 1. He is a former deputy IDF chief and a sitting opposition MK. In July 2026 more than 97,000 members voted in a digital primary (about 86% turnout). Golan also holds reserved appointment slots on the slate.
  • Why it matters: A Democrats slip seats this chair first, and it seats a merger list, not the old Labor or Meretz names alone.
  • Source: JPost / Ynet News, Democrats primary, July 2026.

DEM-M-02 — Naama Lazimi · נעמה לזימי

  • Source: JPost, 2026; Ynet News primary report.

RAM-P-02 — Coalition flexibility, separate from the Joint List

  • Type: Platform · Rarity: Common
  • Subtitle: 2026 structural choice
  • Body: Hadash, Ta'al, and Balad re-formed a Joint List in August 2026. Ra'am is running on its own. Reporting describes Abbas as keeping room to negotiate with more than one bloc, as in 2021. A vote for Ra'am is therefore not a vote for the Joint List’s opposition posture.
  • Why it matters: Same community, two list strategies. The slip you pick is the strategy you seat.
  • Source: JPost 2026 explainer; Times of Israel, Segalovitz announcement.
